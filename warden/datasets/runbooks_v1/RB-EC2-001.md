# RB-EC2-001: Compute bursts and resource hijacking

## Purpose

Applies to a sudden launch of many instances, or of an expensive accelerator class, particularly outside the regions the organisation normally uses. Unauthorised compute is usually cryptomining, and it shows up first as spend. Volume alone proves nothing, since build systems launch fleets routinely.

## Investigation steps

Check the launching principal's baseline for instance families and regions. Compare the launched instance type and region with that history separately: a familiar principal launching an unfamiliar family in a region it never uses is the strongest signal. Look at the launch parameters for user data scripts, and at whether the same principal recently changed its own permissions or had its credentials used from a new network.

## Benign explanations

Continuous-integration systems and batch pipelines launch bursts of short-lived instances, typically in a home region, of a family that appears in their history, with a launch template and a matching termination pattern. Scheduled scale-up before a known event looks similar.

## Containment

Terminate the instances, revoke the credentials used to launch them, and add a region restriction. Terminating compute destroys volatile evidence, so capture instance identifiers and user data first. All of these are write actions needing approval.

## Evidence to capture

The launch events with instance type, region and count, the principal's baseline showing it is or is not typical, and any credential or policy change preceding the launch.
