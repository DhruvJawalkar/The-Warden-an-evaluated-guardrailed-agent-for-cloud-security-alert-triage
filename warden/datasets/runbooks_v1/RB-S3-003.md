# RB-S3-003: Access to data owned by another team

## Purpose

Applies when a principal reads a data store that belongs to a different team than their own, even at low volume. Threshold-based detection misses this by design: one object read never trips a volume rule. Slow, targeted access to sensitive data is the pattern of someone who knows what they want.

## Investigation steps

Establish the data owner and the principal's team from the baseline notes. Check whether the principal has any documented reason to touch this store. Look at the whole window rather than the alerting read: repeated visits over hours, or reads paired with listings, show reconnaissance. Ask what else the principal did nearby, including reads of unrelated sensitive stores and any change to their own access.

## Benign explanations

Cross-functional projects with a recorded access grant, on-call engineers supporting the owning team, and audit staff with read-only remit. The access is usually granted through a documented role and clustered around a known task.

## Containment

Escalate to the data owner and the principal's manager for confirmation before any technical action. If confirmation is not forthcoming, remove the access grant. That is a write action and requires approval.

## Evidence to capture

The individual reads with keys and times, the owner and grant records, and any additional access outside the principal's scope. Slow patterns need every event, not a sample.
