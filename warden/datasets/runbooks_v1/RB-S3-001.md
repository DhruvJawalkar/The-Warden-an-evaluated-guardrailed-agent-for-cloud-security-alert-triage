# RB-S3-001: Storage exposure changes

## Purpose

Applies when a bucket's public access protections are removed or its policy is rewritten. A misconfigured bucket can expose data with no credential at all, so the exposure window matters as much as the intent. Some policy rewrites are deploy automation doing its job; some are the first step of an exfiltration.

## Investigation steps

Read the resulting policy, not just the fact of the change: does it grant to a specific account or to everyone? Check which bucket it is and what it holds; customer data raises severity. Look at who made the change and whether they own the bucket. Search for reads by unauthenticated or unfamiliar sources after the change, which turns exposure into confirmed access.

## Benign explanations

Infrastructure-as-code deployments replace bucket policies as a matter of course, usually with a scoped principal, on a bucket the deploying role owns, and in the deploy window. Static-website buckets are meant to be readable.

## Containment

Restore the public access block and revert the policy to its last known good version. Both are write actions and need approval. Record the exposure window from the change until reversal so that data owners can assess what may have been read.

## Evidence to capture

The policy change with the new policy document, the access-block removal, and any anonymous or foreign reads after it. For a benign call, the deployment event that explains the change.
