# RB-GEN-002: Evidence standards for a verdict

## Purpose

Defines what makes a triage verdict defensible. A correct label reached without its supporting events is a lucky guess, and the next reader cannot verify it. Every verdict cites the events that establish it.

## Investigation steps

Before concluding, retrieve the full record of every event you intend to cite; search summaries omit the request parameters and resources that carry the meaning. Include the earliest event in the chain, not only the most dramatic one, since the start of an attack often looks mundane. When several principals are involved, cite at least one event per principal.

## Benign explanations

For a false positive, cite events that affirmatively show legitimacy: the scheduled job record, the ticketed change, the sign-in from the known network. Do not cite the absence of alerts. If you cannot point to a positive record, the honest verdict is that the activity is unexplained.

## Escalation

State the confidence and what would change your mind. If the investigation ran out of budget, say the work is unfinished instead of guessing, because a guessed closure can bury an intrusion and a guessed escalation wastes someone's day.

## Evidence to capture

Full records of cited events, the timeline linking them, and the baseline comparison. Keep the rationale short and refer to events by identifier.
